  ## 1. The core idea                                                           
                                                                                
  A voice-based learning agent that does not teach first. It diagnoses first.   
                                                                                
  The insight driving this: when you try to learn something hard on your own,   
  the hardest part is not finding information. It is that you do not know what  
  you do not know, so you cannot ask the right questions. Generic chat          
  assistants answer whatever you ask, which means they are useless exactly when 
  you cannot formulate the question.                                            
                                                                                
  The agent's job is to find your **frontier**: the point where your            
  understanding stops and the confusion begins. Then it works from there, one   
  prerequisite at a time.                                                       
                                                                                
  Chosen domain: **machine learning**. Good pick because the prerequisite       
  chains are long and brutal, and almost everyone learns it out of order.       
  People nod along about backpropagation without solid calculus intuition, and  
  the gap compounds silently for years.                                         
                                                                                
  **The valuable artifact is not the conversation. It is the map.** Showing     
  someone the terrain, where they are on it, and the single node blocking them. 
  That is the thing generic ChatGPT structurally cannot do, because it has no   
  persistent model of the learner.                                              
                                                                                
  ---                                                                           
                                                                                
  ## 2. Competitive position (researched, not assumed)                          
                                                                                
  ### What exists                                                               
                                                                                
  **Academic / research side is crowded.** Knowledge-graph-plus-RAG tutoring    
  systems are a well-published area. Deep Knowledge Tracing (DKT), Bayesian     
  Knowledge Tracing (BKT), Item Response Theory (IRT), curriculum-aware         
  cognitive diagnosis via GNNs. There is even an open-source self-hosted        
  platform doing knowledge graphs plus teach-back evaluation.                   
                                                                                
  **Funded startups are almost entirely institutional or exam-driven:**         
                                                                                
  | Player | Focus | Why not a competitor |                                     
  |---|---|---|                                                                 
  | Alice (YC) | "Duolingo for exams", college students | Exam prep, fixed      
  syllabus |                                                                    
  | Codingal (YC) | K-12 programming, live instructors | Kids, human-led |      
  | Multiverse | Apprenticeships, enterprise | B2B |                            
  | Pluralsight /                                                               
  ──── (151 lines hidden) ───────────────────────────────────────────────────── 
  al target user knows less and gets stuck in different places.                 
                                                                                
  Mitigation: test with at least a few people who are genuinely mid-struggle    
  with ML, and watch where they stall rather than asking them if they liked it. 
                                                                                
  ### 6.4 Thin moat on the conversation itself                                  
                                                                                
  The diagnostic prompt is copyable. Anyone can write a similar one.            
                                                                                
  What is actually defensible is the **persistent learner model** (tracking     
  understanding across weeks and adapting) plus **well-curated graph            
  structure**. Both are the unglamorous parts most people skip. The POC does    
  not need to solve this, but it should not architect in a way that makes       
  persistence hard to add later.                                                
                                                                                
  ### 6.5 Model quality variance under BYOK                                     
                                                                                
  Partially mitigated by whitelisting (5.2) and action constraints (5.1).       
  Residual risk remains and is accepted.                                        
                                                                                
  ---                                                                           
                                                                                
  ## 7. Known vs unknown                                                        
                                                                                
  ### Decided                                                                   
                                                                                
  - Domain: machine learning, one narrow chain for the POC                      
  - Diagnostic-first, not teaching-first                                        
  - Graph as static hand-authored JSON, no database in v1                       
  - BYOK for API access                                                         
  - Voice is essential, delivered via assembled pipeline not realtime API       
  - Model whitelist enforced                                                    
  - Agent constrained to a fixed action set                                     
  - Success metric: does the rendered map produce recognition in a real user    
                                                                                
  ### Open                                                                      
                                                                                
  - Which specific TTS provider, if the browser default proves too robotic      
  - Exact node count and where the chain starts and ends                        
  - How mastery state is represented: binary known/unknown, or graded           
  confidence                                                                    
  - How probe questions are authored: hand-written per node, or generated once  
  and reviewed                                                                  
  - Whether the learner model persists across sessions in the POC or resets     
  each time                                                                     
  - Graph visualization library                                                 
  - Whether the frontier is a single node or a set of nodes                     
                                                                                
  ### Explicitly out of scope for the POC                                       
                                                                                
  - Multiple domains                                                            
  - Accounts and authentication                                                 
  - Payments                                                                    
  - Mobile                                                                      
  - Teaching quality optimization                                               
  - Deep knowledge tracing or any learned mastery model. Hand-authored          
  heuristics only at this stage.                                                
                                                                                
  ---                                                                           
                                                                                
  ## 8. Suggested build order                                                   
                                                                                
  1. Author the graph JSON. Do this first and by hand. It is the product.       
  2. Build the graph renderer with fake state hardcoded. Confirm the map is     
  legible and produces a reaction before wiring anything up.                    
  3. Text-only diagnostic loop with the constrained action set. Prove placement 
  works.                                                                        
  4. Add the voice layer on top of the working text loop.                       
  5. BYOK key handling and model whitelist.                                     
                                                                                
  Voice comes fourth deliberately. It is essential to the product but not to    
  validating whether the diagnostic works.

     ---

     The consequence of "free fallback": there has to be a server

     The pasted spec (§3) assumed keys live client-side and the browser calls
     the provider
     directly. A free fallback breaks that. The fallback runs on the owner's
     key, and an
     owner key shipped to the browser is stolen within a day of the app being
     public.

     So one Next route handler, src/app/api/turn/route.ts, handles both paths:

     browser ──POST /api/turn──▶ route handler ──▶ provider
                                  │
                                  ├─ request carries a user key  → use it, in
     memory only,
                                  │                                never logged,
     never stored
                                  └─ no user key                 → owner's
     Google free-tier key,
                                                                   hard-capped
     (see below)

     Two paths through one seam, rather than two transports. It also makes the
     model whitelist
     actually enforceable — a client-side whitelist is a suggestion.

     The trade-off, stated plainly: BYOK keys now transit the server, which
     gives up the
     "your key never touches my server" story the pasted spec was reaching for.
     That story was
     already gone the moment a free fallback was required, so paying for it
     twice with two code
     paths buys nothing. The UI should say what happens to the key: used for
     this request, held
     in memory, never written down.

     Next.js 16 (App Router) · React 19 · TypeScript · Tailwind 4 · shadcn/ui

     Next.js rather than a bare SPA because it matches interview-loop exactly —
     every gotcha in

     shadcn-on-Base-UI config is
     copyable, and it deploys free on Vercel Hobby. There is no src/proxy.ts and
     no password

     Role: BYOK upgrade

     This reframes spec §5.2. The spec said "whitelist frontier models" because
     a small model
     gives a wandering tutor. The constrained action set (§5.1) is what makes a
     cheap model
     viable — it picks one of four actions against a strict schema, it does not
     invent a
     curriculum. So the whitelist becomes a tested list rather than a frontier
     list, and
     §5 below is what earns a model its place on it. A model that has not been
     calibrated does
     not go in the list.

     @ai-sdk/openai is a near-free addition the AI SDK makes trivial, but every
     added provider
     needs its own calibration run, so v1 is Google only.

     Capping the free fallback

     Google AI Studio's free tier is ~1,500 requests/day shared across every 
     user of the
     project, and per-model quotas differ wildly. At ~20 turns per placement
     session that is
     roughly 75 sessions/day in total, and one enthusiastic user can eat a real
     share of it.

     With no database in v1, there is no honest per-user counter. So:

     - Hard per-session turn cap (~25), enforced server-side via a signed
     session token
     (jose, already a dependency in interview-loop) — not client-side, which is
     trivially
     bypassed.
     - Degrade, never crash. Port isQuotaError from
     interview-loop/src/lib/models.ts
     verbatim; it walks the whole cause chain because the AI SDK wraps a 429 in
     AI_NoOutputGeneratedError, whose message mentions neither quota nor a
     status code. The
     first fallback implementation there silently never fired for exactly this
     reason.
     - On exhaustion: "The shared free tier is used up for today — add your own 
     key to
     continue." Not a stack trace.
     - maxRetries: 0 on model calls, as in the sibling: the SDK default of 3
     burns three
     requests of a small allowance against a model that already said no.

     tables from

     The misconceptions are the highest-value authoring

     This domain was chosen partly because its popular explanations are so
     reliably wrong. Each
     is a misconceptions entry and a probe target:

     - neuron — "it's like a brain cell." It is a weighted sum and a threshold.
     No chemistry.
     - next-token-prediction — "it just predicts the next word, so it can't
     reason." Confuses
     the training objective with a capability ceiling. The most consequential 
     one.
     - attention — "it pays attention to important words." Anthropomorphic; it
     is a learned
     weighted average.
     - embeddings — "similar words sit close together." True, but the 2D plots
     people have seen
     are projections that destroy nearly all the structure.
     - gradient-descent — "a ball rolling down a hill." Implies you can see the
     landscape and
     that it has three dimensions.
     - hallucination — "the model lies / makes things up." Implies intent, and
     implies a
     truthful mode it is departing from.
     - parameters-scale — "more parameters means smarter." Conflates capacity
     with capability.
     - agents — "an AI that does things on its own." Hides the loop and the
     tools; it is the
     same next-token model in a while.

     Cross-check the prerequisite edges against an established curriculum before
     wiring
     anything up (spec risk 6.2 — a confidently-wrong map is worse than a vague
     one).

     3. The diagnostic loop

     src/lib/agent/decide.ts, called from the route handler. One structured
     decision per turn
     via the AI SDK's generateObject with a Zod discriminated union — not tool
     calling.
     There is no tool to execute; we want exactly one decision,
     schema-validated.

     type Action =
       | { kind: 'probe';   nodeId: string; say: string }
       | { kind: 'explain'; nodeId: string; say: string }
       | { kind: 'mark';    nodeId: string; state: NodeState; because: string }
       | { kind: 'advance'; say: string };

     The model's freedom is in say, never in direction. Send it only the current
     node, its
     prerequisites, and the learner state — not all 23 nodes. Cheaper, and it is
     the main defence
     against drift (spec §5.1).

     ⚠️ Reasoning tokens count against maxOutputTokens. Gemini models think
     before
     answering; measured in interview-loop, a 220-token cap left
     gemini-3.6-flash with 212
     tokens of thinking and four characters of output. Budget ≥900 and enforce
     brevity in the
     prompt instead.

  The claim being tested

  What those npx vercel commands actually do

  No, nothing is copied automatically. Neither command touches your clipboard,
  your .env.local, or any file.

  npx vercel env rm GOOGLE_GENERATIVE_AI_API_KEY production --scope
  willcraigzs-projects
  Deletes that variable from Vercel's production environment. Prompts you to
  confirm. Doesn't touch anything on your machine.

  npx vercel env add GOOGLE_GENERATIVE_AI_API_KEY production --scope
  willcraigzs-projects
  Prints ? What's the value of GOOGLE_GENERATIVE_AI_API_KEY? and waits. You 
  paste the new key at that prompt — ⌘V, Enter. It reads stdin, sends it to
  Vercel, and stores it encrypted. Nothing is read from disk.

  One thing to know: the change doesn't take effect until you redeploy. Env vars
  are baked in at build time.

  npx vercel deploy --prod --yes --scope willcraigzs-projects

  Your local .env.local still has the old key, so dev keeps using that. Update
  it by hand if you want them matched.

  The hook

  ~/.claude/settings.json — global, so it applies to every Claude Code session
  on this machine.

  It intercepts Read, Edit, Write, Grep, Glob and Bash and asks before anything
  touches .env*, .envrc, credentials, .netrc, id_rsa, .pem, .ssh/, .aws/,
  .npmrc, or secrets.*. That includes indirect routes — cat .env.local | pbcopy
  is caught as a Bash command, not just a file read.

  Verified before wiring it in:

  ┌─────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────┐
  │              Asks               │      Passes through       │
  ├─────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤
  │ .env.local, .env.production     │ .env.example, .env.sample │
  ├─────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤
  │ cat .env.local | pbcopy         │ pnpm test, git status     │
  ├─────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤
  │ ~/.aws/credentials, .ssh/id_rsa │ environment.ts            │
  └─────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┘

  Two deliberate choices:

  It asks rather than denies. A hard block would make legitimate work impossible
  and you'd end up turning it off. A prompt costs one keystroke and keeps the
  decision yours.

  .env.example is explicitly allowed. It's a template with no values, committed
  on purpose — blocking it would train you to click through the prompt without
  reading it, which defeats the whole thing.

  The prompt says: "This call touches a secrets file. Approve only if you meant
